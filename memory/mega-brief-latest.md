## Scholarships: Finding & Winning Aid

### Key Tips
- **Start Early** – Begin researching in 10th/11th grade; deadlines appear year-round.
- **Cast a Wide Net** – Look at school, local, state, national, and private sources.
- **Stay Organized** – Track deadlines, requirements, and submitted materials in a spreadsheet.
- **Tailor Applications** – Match your essay and materials to each scholarship’s mission.
- **Proofread Everything** – Typos can disqualify an otherwise strong application.
- **Apply for Large & Small Awards** – Smaller scholarships are less competitive and add up.
- **Leverage Your Story** – Use personal experiences, passions, and challenges to stand out.
- **Don’t Discount “Underrated” Scholarships** – Niche awards often have fewer applicants.
- **Reuse & Adapt** – Save essays and adapt them for multiple prompts.
- **Stay Persistent** – Apply regularly; winning often comes from volume and improvement.

### Where to Search
- **Federal Student Aid** – studentaid.gov (scholarship search tool)
- **Scholarship Databases** – Scholarships.com, Fastweb, Cappex, Niche
- **College Financial Aid Offices** – Institutional aid specific to each school
- **Community Organizations** – Local businesses, foundations, churches, clubs
- **Employer Programs** – Parent’s workplace or your own part‑time job may offer aid
- **Professional Associations** – Groups related to your intended major/career

### Common Requirements
- Completed application form
- Official transcripts
- Letters of recommendation (teacher, counselor, employer)
- Personal statement or essay (often 500‑1,000 words)
- Financial need documentation (FAFSA, tax returns) for need‑based awards
- Proof of eligibility (e.g., ethnicity, field of study, community service)

## Admissions: Navigating the Application Process

### Timeline Overview
- **Spring/Summer Junior Year** – Research colleges, visit campuses, begin test prep.
- **Fall Senior Year** – Finalize college list, request recommendations, draft essays.
- **Early Action/Early Decision** – Typically November deadlines; decisions by December.
- **Regular Decision** – Usually January‑February deadlines; decisions March‑April.
- **Rolling Admissions** – Apply early for best chance; review continues until spots fill.

### Application Components
1. **College List** – Mix of reach, match, and safety schools (academically & financially).
2. **Transcripts** – Official high school record; request early.
3. **Standardized Tests** – SAT/ACT (optional at many schools); check each college’s policy.
4. **Letters of Recommendation** – 2‑3 from teachers who know your academic strengths.
5. **Personal Essay** – 650‑word Common App essay or school‑specific prompts.
6. **Activities List** – Extracurriculars, work, volunteering, leadership roles.
7. **Supplemental Essays** – Short answers specific to each college’s values/programs.
8. **Portfolio/Audition** – Required for arts, architecture, music, or specialized programs.
9. **Interviews** – Optional or recommended; prepare to discuss fit and goals.

### Tips for a Strong Application
- **Be Authentic** – Admissions officers seek genuine voice, not a fabricated persona.
- **Show Impact** – Quantify achievements (e.g., “raised $2,000 for charity,” “led team of 10”).
- **Demonstrate Fit** – Reference specific professors, courses, or opportunities at each college.
- **Keep It Well‑Written** – Proofread essays; consider a teacher or counselor review.
- **Meet Deadlines** – Submit early to avoid technical glitches and show enthusiasm.
- **Utilize Fee Waivers** – Eligible via College Board, NACAC, or individual college programs.
- **Check Requirements Grid** – Use the Common App requirements PDF to track each school’s supplements.
- **Consider Demonstrated Interest** – Attend virtual tours, webinars, or contact admissions (if tracked).

### Resources
- **Common App** – commonapp.org (account rollover, requirements grid, essay prompts)
- **College Essay Guy** – collegeessayguy.com (guides, brainstorming, essay reviews)
- **Princeton Review College Advice** – princetonreview.com/college-advice

- **Reddit r/ApplyingToCollege** – peer advice, timelines, essay feedback
- **YouTube Walkthroughs** – Search “Common App walkthrough 2025” for visual guides

## Programs: Choosing a Major & Academic Path

### How to Select a Major
1. **Self‑Assessment** – Reflect on subjects you enjoy, problems you like solving, and values.
2. **Explore Intro Courses** – Many colleges allow undecided first year; use electives to sample fields.
3. **Research Career Outcomes** – Look at typical jobs, salary data, and growth for majors of interest.
4. **Talk to Advisors & Professors** – Seek insight on coursework, research opportunities, and internships.
5. **Consider Flexibility** – Some majors (e.g., business, communications) offer broad applicability.
6. **Think About Graduate School** – Certain fields require advanced degrees for career advancement.
7. **Check Program Strength** – Investigate faculty, research labs, internship placements, and alumni networks.
8. **Stay Open to Change** – It’s common to switch majors; pick a school that makes shifting easy.

### Popular & Emerging Fields (2024‑2025 Trends)
- **Computer Science / AI** – High demand across industries; look for strong research labs.
- **Health Sciences** – Nursing, public health, biomedical sciences driven by aging population.
- **Environmental Studies / Sustainability** – Growth in green energy, conservation, policy roles.
- **Data Science & Analytics** – Interdisciplinary; combines stats, coding, domain knowledge.
- **Engineering (Renewable Energy, Robotics)** – Applied problem‑solving with clear career paths.
- **Psychology & Neuroscience** – Expanding into mental health, UX research, cognitive science.
- **Business & Entrepreneurship** – Versatile; look for incubators, pitch competitions, internships.
- **Liberal Arts Interdisciplinary** – Programs that let you design your own major (e.g., PPE, ISTEP).

### Resources for Major Exploration
- **Princeton Review Guide to Choosing College Majors** – princetonreview.com/college-advice/choosing-college-majors
- **McMillan Education Blog** – mcmillaneducation.com/blog/how-to-choose-a-college-major/
- **University Department Websites** – Review required courses, faculty bios, and student projects.
- **Informational Interviews** – Talk to professionals or upperclassmen in fields of interest.
- **Internships & Job Shadowing** – Short‑term experiences to test day‑to‑day work.
- **MOOCs & Online Courses** – Coursera, edX, Khan Academy to sample college‑level material.
